# Separate Grammatical Relations from Semantic Relations

Grammatical Relations are a separate Dumrel domain. `CaseCounterpart` connects
forms that vary along case while preserving a grammatical reference;
`PersonCounterpart` connects forms that vary along person while preserving the
other authored coordinates. `NumberCounterpart` connects exact Readings whose
singular/plural learner identities were deliberately promoted by a reviewed
Fixed Population while preserving the other authored coordinates. All three
relations are symmetric, non-transitive, and non-substitutive.

`NumberCounterpart` is promotion-only. It applies to the reviewed German total
PRON split `alles` and `alle` and the reviewed formal singular/plural addressee
splits of `Sie`, `Ihnen`, and `Ihrer`; identical spelling does not collapse the
exact Reading endpoints. It also applies within the independently reviewed
demonstrative and relative exact-form `der/die/das` populations, where each
singular Case/Gender branch is compared with its plural peer and duplicate or
self pairs are discarded. It is not inferred between otherwise independent
personal or possessive identities from referent or possessor number. Ordinary
pairs such as `ich` and `wir`, `du` and `ihr`, or `mein` and `unser` remain
unresolved until separately reviewed. A Number Series classifies grammatical
peers and never implies a Semantic Synonym or contextual substitutability.

One claim has homogeneous Lemma endpoints or homogeneous exact Reading
endpoints. A Grammatical Series authors members along one axis and compiles to
unique direct peer claims without self-edges. Series overlap does not imply
closure across Series.

Hosts persist only direct claims. Reverse navigation is a projected symmetric
view and exact Reading endpoints remain exact Reading links. Grammatical
Relations do not enter Reading Knowledge, Semantic Relation settings,
propagation, pending-target resolution, synonym substitution, or semantic
closure.
